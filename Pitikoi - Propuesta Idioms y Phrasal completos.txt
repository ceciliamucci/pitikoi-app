A. IDIOMS & EXPRESSIONS — LISTA IMPLEMENTADA
============================================================

L01. BIG DEAL
Meaning: something important or serious
Español: algo importante / gran cosa
Example: "Relax. It's not a big deal. We can fix it."
Pitikoi Tip: Tone matters. "Getting the job was a big deal for her" describes something important. A sarcastic "Big deal" can mean "So what?" and may sound dismissive.

L02. MAKE SENSE
Meaning: to be understandable, logical or reasonable
Español: tener sentido / ser comprensible
Example: "Oh, the entrance is round the back? Okay, that makes sense."
Pitikoi Tip: Common patterns: "Does that make sense?", "That makes no sense" and "It makes sense now."

L03. ASAP
Meaning: as soon as possible
Español: lo antes posible / cuanto antes
Example: "Could you send me the address ASAP? The taxi's almost here."
Pitikoi Tip: ASAP means as soon as possible rather than giving a specific deadline. It can sound demanding, so a polite question or a clear deadline may be better in some situations.

L04. SO FAR, SO GOOD
Meaning: things have gone well up to this point
Español: hasta ahora, todo bien
Example: "How's the new job?" "So far, so good. Everyone's been really helpful."
Pitikoi Tip: The situation is still in progress. This is not a promise that everything will keep going well.

L05. A LAST RESORT
Meaning: an option you use only when other options have failed or are unavailable
Español: un último recurso
Example: "I'd only replace the laptop as a last resort."
Pitikoi Tip: The usual pattern is "as a last resort". It is the fallback, not the first choice.

L06. GIVE SOMEONE A RUN FOR THEIR MONEY
Meaning: to compete strongly enough to make someone work hard to win
Español: dar pelea / ser un rival difícil
Example: "We lost, but we really gave the champions a run for their money."
Pitikoi Tip: It usually has nothing to do with literal money. You can give someone strong competition without beating them.

L07. COME FULL CIRCLE
Meaning: to return to a similar starting point after a series of changes
Español: cerrar un círculo / volver al punto de partida tras un recorrido
Example: "I learned to cook in this kitchen. Now I'm teaching my daughter here. It feels like I've come full circle."
Pitikoi Tip: There is a connection back to the beginning. Simply finishing a task is not necessarily coming full circle.

L08. COME OUT OF LEFT FIELD
Meaning: to be unexpected, often also odd or unrelated to what was happening
Español: salir de la nada / resultar inesperado y a menudo desconcertante
Example: "We were talking about dinner when he asked if I believed in ghosts. That came out of left field."
Pitikoi Tip: "Out of the blue" emphasizes an unexpected event. "Out of left field" often adds a feeling of strangeness or a sudden change of topic. They can overlap; the distinction is not absolute.

L09. KNOCK IT OUT OF THE PARK
Meaning: to do something extremely well
Español: romperla / hacerlo extraordinariamente bien
Example: "You knocked it out of the park with that presentation."
Pitikoi Tip: It comes from baseball but is often used to praise work, performances or other achievements.

L10. FAIR ENOUGH
Meaning: a response that accepts someone's reason, opinion or decision as reasonable
Español: está bien / es razonable / se entiende
Example: "I'm staying home. I've barely slept." "Fair enough. Get some rest."
Pitikoi Tip: You can accept someone's reason without sharing their preference or agreeing with everything they say.

L11. MY BAD
Meaning: an informal way to admit that a mistake was yours
Español: fue mi culpa / me equivoqué
Example: "My bad. I thought you said Friday."
Pitikoi Tip: Casual and common for small mistakes. It may sound too light for serious harm or a formal apology.

L12. NO WORRIES
Meaning: a casual way to say that something is okay or is not a problem
Español: no pasa nada / no te preocupes / de nada, según el contexto
Example: "Sorry I'm a bit late." "No worries. We haven't ordered yet."
Pitikoi Tip: It can respond to an apology, a request or thanks. With a serious concern, 'no worries' can sometimes sound dismissive depending on the context and tone.

L13. YOU NEVER KNOW
Meaning: something is possible even if it seems unlikely or uncertain
Español: nunca se sabe / quién te dice
Example: "I'll apply anyway. You never know — they might like my portfolio."
Pitikoi Tip: It leaves room for a possibility; it does not promise a good result.

L14. IT IS WHAT IT IS
Meaning: accepting a situation you cannot change, even if you do not like it
Español: es lo que hay
Example: "We've missed the last train. It is what it is. Let's find a bus."
Pitikoi Tip: It can express resignation. Said to someone who needs support, it may sound dismissive.

L15. NOT GONNA LIE
Meaning: an informal way to introduce an honest opinion or admission
Español: no te voy a mentir / la verdad
Example: "Not gonna lie, I actually loved that terrible movie."
Pitikoi Tip: Gonna is informal for going to. This expression adds an honest, sometimes surprising reaction; it is not normally an admission of earlier lying.

L16. AT THE END OF THE DAY
Meaning: when you consider what matters most
Español: al fin y al cabo / en definitiva
Example: "The pay's better there, but at the end of the day, I want a job I actually enjoy."
Pitikoi Tip: In this use it summarizes the main point. It does not mean a literal time in the evening.

L17. FOR THE TIME BEING
Meaning: for now, until the situation changes
Español: por el momento / por ahora
Example: "I'm staying with my sister for the time being."
Pitikoi Tip: The arrangement is temporary, but its exact end date may be unknown.

L18. ON THE SAME PAGE
Meaning: sharing an understanding of a plan or situation
Español: estar en sintonía / tener el mismo entendimiento
Example: "So we're meeting at six, outside the station? Good, we're on the same page."
Pitikoi Tip: It often means agreement or shared understanding about a specific matter, not identical opinions about everything.

L19. OUT OF THE BLUE
Meaning: unexpectedly, without warning
Español: de la nada / inesperadamente
Example: "She texted me out of the blue after three years."
Pitikoi Tip: Unlike "out of left field", this does not necessarily suggest an odd or unrelated topic. An ordinary message can arrive unexpectedly.

L20. THE OTHER WAY AROUND
Meaning: with the direction, order or relationship reversed
Español: al revés / a la inversa
Example: "You think I borrowed her jacket? It was the other way around — she borrowed mine."
Pitikoi Tip: It reverses the relationship; it does not just introduce an unrelated alternative.

L21. EASIER SAID THAN DONE
Meaning: something sounds simple but is difficult to do
Español: es más fácil decirlo que hacerlo
Example: "'Just stop checking your phone.' Easier said than done."
Pitikoi Tip: It points out a practical difficulty. It does not necessarily mean the advice is wrong or the action is impossible.

L22. BETTER SAFE THAN SORRY
Meaning: it is worth taking a precaution to avoid a problem later
Español: más vale prevenir que lamentar
Example: "I'll save a copy before I update it. Better safe than sorry."
Pitikoi Tip: Usually said before a possible problem, to justify a precaution.

L23. GO WITH THE FLOW
Meaning: to accept how things develop instead of trying to control every detail
Español: dejarse llevar / adaptarse a lo que surja
Example: "We don't have a plan for Saturday. We'll just go with the flow."
Pitikoi Tip: It suggests flexibility, not that you must accept something unsafe or uncomfortable.

L24. GET THE HANG OF IT
Meaning: to learn how to do something through practice
Español: agarrarle la mano / aprender cómo se hace
Example: "The controls felt weird at first, but I'm getting the hang of it."
Pitikoi Tip: It focuses on becoming familiar with a skill or process, not necessarily mastering it completely.

L25. CALL IT A DAY
Meaning: to stop an activity for the day, or end a session
Español: dar por terminado por hoy / dejarlo por hoy
Example: "We've done enough. Let's call it a day."
Pitikoi Tip: Often used for work or practice. It does not normally mean quitting the activity permanently.

B. GUESS THE MEANING — 12 EJERCICIOS DISPONIBLES
============================================================
Presentación: expresión destacada + contexto + "What do you think it means here?"
Cada ejercicio usa una expresión diferente.
Correcta: mostrar "Exactly!" y la explicación.
Incorrecta: mostrar "Not quite.", el feedback específico, la respuesta correcta y la explicación.
Tip: reutilizar la entrada de la lista indicada, sin mantener otra versión del mismo tip.

G01. OUT OF THE BLUE
Context:
"I hadn't heard from Leo in three years. Then he called me out of the blue yesterday to ask how I was."
A. He called unexpectedly.
B. He called because he was feeling sad.
C. He called at a time they had agreed on.
D. He called to continue a recent conversation.
Correcta: A.
Explicación: The long silence and sudden call suggest there was no warning. "Out of the blue" means unexpectedly.
Feedback B: Blue can describe sadness, but nothing here tells us how Leo felt. The expression describes the unexpected call.
Feedback C: There is no planned call; the speaker emphasizes the surprise after years without contact.
Feedback D: They had not spoken for years, so this was not a continuation of a recent conversation.
Tip: L19.

G02. BIG DEAL
Context:
"Getting that scholarship was a big deal for me. Without it, I couldn't have afforded to study."
A. It was a particularly good financial offer.
B. It was something very important.
C. It was a small benefit that barely mattered.
D. It was a problem that delayed studying.
Correcta: B.
Explicación: The scholarship made studying possible, so it was genuinely important. There is no sarcastic tone here.
Feedback A: Deal can mean an agreement, but this sentence explains the scholarship's importance, not the quality of a financial offer.
Feedback C: The speaker could not have studied without it, so it was not a minor benefit.
Feedback D: It enabled the studies rather than delaying them.
Tip: L01.

G03. GIVE SOMEONE A RUN FOR THEIR MONEY
Context:
"We lost 3–2, but the champions had to fight for every point. We really gave them a run for their money."
A. We paid them to play against us.
B. We beat them by a large margin.
C. We made it difficult for them to win.
D. We helped them win without much effort.
Correcta: C.
Explicación: A close score and a hard-fought match show strong competition. You do not need to win to give someone a run for their money.
Feedback A: This is about competition, not a payment.
Feedback B: The speaker explicitly says their team lost.
Feedback D: The champions had to fight for every point, so their opponents did not make it easy.
Tip: L06.

G04. COME FULL CIRCLE
Context:
"I used to come to this club for my first guitar lessons. Twenty years later, I'm back here teaching beginners. I've come full circle."
A. I've stopped being interested in music.
B. I've repeated the same lesson without improving.
C. I've finally finished every course the club offers.
D. I've returned to a place connected to where my journey began.
Correcta: D.
Explicación: The speaker has returned to the original club in a new role after a long journey.
Feedback A: Teaching guitar shows continued involvement in music.
Feedback B: The speaker has changed from beginner to teacher, not stayed at the same level.
Feedback C: There is no claim about completing all the club's courses. The key is returning to the beginning in a changed situation.
Tip: L07.

G05. KNOCK IT OUT OF THE PARK
Context:
"Your demo was brilliant. Even the client who was unsure wants to sign up now. You knocked it out of the park."
A. You did an excellent job.
B. You took a risk that went badly.
C. You moved the event to a different location.
D. You completed the minimum that was required.
Correcta: A.
Explicación: Brilliant and the client's enthusiastic response show outstanding performance, not just an acceptable result.
Feedback B: The client was convinced, so the outcome was positive.
Feedback C: Park is part of the idiom; the speaker is praising the demo, not describing a move.
Feedback D: This praise is much stronger than saying the basic requirements were met.
Tip: L09.

G06. FAIR ENOUGH
Context:
"'I'm not coming out. I've got an early shift.' 'Fair enough. Get some sleep — we'll catch up another time.'"
A. The speaker thinks the shift should be cancelled.
B. The speaker accepts the reason for staying home.
C. The speaker is asking for a better excuse.
D. The speaker insists that the friend come out anyway.
Correcta: B.
Explicación: The reply accepts the friend's reason and suggests meeting another time.
Feedback A: Nobody proposes changing the work shift.
Feedback C: Get some sleep shows acceptance, not a demand for a stronger excuse.
Feedback D: We'll catch up another time respects the decision to stay home.
Tip: L10.

G07. YOU NEVER KNOW
Context:
"'They'll probably choose someone with more experience.' 'Maybe. Apply anyway. You never know — they might love your ideas.'"
A. The application is guaranteed to succeed.
B. Nobody is allowed to explain the selection process.
C. There is still a chance, despite the uncertainty.
D. There is no point applying without experience.
Correcta: C.
Explicación: The speaker leaves room for a positive possibility without promising it.
Feedback A: Might signals possibility, not certainty.
Feedback B: The conversation concerns the outcome, not restrictions on sharing information.
Feedback D: Apply anyway is encouragement to try, not advice to give up.
Tip: L13.

G08. AT THE END OF THE DAY
Context:
"That flat's bigger, but it's miles from everyone I know. At the end of the day, being near my friends matters more to me."
A. The speaker can only view the flat in the evening.
B. The flat is closer to friends after work.
C. The speaker has decided that size is the only important factor.
D. The speaker is identifying what matters most overall.
Correcta: D.
Explicación: The speaker weighs different factors, then states the priority that matters most.
Feedback A: The phrase introduces a conclusion, not an appointment time.
Feedback B: Distance does not change after work; the speaker is discussing priorities.
Feedback C: The speaker explicitly values being near friends more than having a bigger flat.
Tip: L16.

G09. FOR THE TIME BEING
Context:
"My flat's being repaired, so I'm staying with my brother for the time being. I'll move back when it's ready."
A. The arrangement is temporary.
B. The speaker has moved in permanently.
C. The speaker has not moved in yet.
D. The arrangement has already ended.
Correcta: A.
Explicación: The speaker is staying there now and expects to leave when the repairs are finished.
Feedback B: I'll move back shows the arrangement is not permanent.
Feedback C: I'm staying says the arrangement is already happening.
Feedback D: The speaker is still living with the brother while the flat is repaired.
Tip: L17.

G10. THE OTHER WAY AROUND
Context:
"'Did you teach your dad to cook?' 'Actually, it was the other way around. He taught me everything.'"
A. They both learned from the same person.
B. The roles were reversed: the dad was the teacher.
C. They changed the order of the recipes.
D. Neither of them knew how to cook.
Correcta: B.
Explicación: The correction reverses who taught whom.
Feedback A: The dad taught the speaker; no shared teacher is mentioned.
Feedback C: The reversal concerns the people and their roles, not recipe order.
Feedback D: He taught me everything tells us the dad knew how to cook.
Tip: L20.

G11. EASIER SAID THAN DONE
Context:
"'Just tell your boss you've got too much work.' 'Easier said than done. She gets annoyed whenever anyone says no.'"
A. The conversation with the boss has already happened.
B. The advice is completely impossible to follow.
C. The suggestion sounds simple but would be difficult to carry out.
D. Speaking to the boss will be easier than expected.
Correcta: C.
Explicación: The boss's reaction creates a practical difficulty. The phrase does not say the conversation is impossible.
Feedback A: The speaker describes an obstacle, not a completed conversation.
Feedback B: Difficult is not the same as impossible; the expression questions how easy the advice is to follow.
Feedback D: The speaker expects difficulty, not an unexpectedly easy conversation.
Tip: L21.

G12. GET THE HANG OF IT
Context:
"I kept missing the turns in this game yesterday. Now I can finish a lap without crashing. I'm getting the hang of it."
A. The speaker has decided to stop playing.
B. The speaker understands the rules but has made no progress.
C. The speaker is already the best player.
D. The speaker is learning how to play through practice.
Correcta: D.
Explicación: Fewer mistakes after practice show growing familiarity with the controls.
Feedback A: The speaker is still playing and improving.
Feedback B: Finishing a lap without crashing is clear progress.
Feedback C: Improvement does not imply complete mastery or being better than everyone else.
Tip: L24.

C. CHOOSE THE SITUATION — 12 EJERCICIOS DISPONIBLES
============================================================
Presentación: expresión + "Where would this response fit best?"
Las opciones describen situaciones y la intención del hablante. No incluyen la respuesta modelo antes de elegir.
El feedback explica adecuación pragmática, no solo traducción.

S01. SO FAR, SO GOOD
A. Your course finished yesterday. A friend asks whether you passed the final exam.
B. You're two weeks into a new job. A friend asks how it's going, and things have been fine.
C. You start a new job next month. A friend asks what you expect it to be like.
Correcta: B.
Respuesta modelo: "So far, so good. The team's been really helpful."
Explicación: The job is already in progress, and the speaker is reporting a positive experience up to now.
Feedback A: The course has ended. This calls for the final result rather than an update on something ongoing.
Feedback C: The job has not started, so there is no experience so far to report.
Tip: L04.

S02. MY BAD
A. You realize you sent a friend the wrong address. You want to own up to the mix-up.
B. Your friend sent you the wrong address and apologizes. You want to reassure them that it's okay.
C. Your friend asks which address is correct, and you're certain you sent the right one.
Correcta: A.
Respuesta modelo: "My bad — I sent you the old one."
Explicación: The speaker admits responsibility for their own small mistake.
Feedback B: The mistake belongs to the friend. A reassuring reply such as no worries would fit better.
Feedback C: There is no mistake to admit in this situation.
Tip: L11.

S03. A LAST RESORT
A. You choose your favourite restaurant first because you know you'll enjoy it.
B. You have several equally good dinner options left and choose one without any particular preference.
C. Every nearby restaurant is closed and there's nothing to cook. You consider the expensive hotel restaurant only because nothing else is available.
Correcta: C.
Respuesta modelo: "We could eat at the hotel as a last resort."
Explicación: The hotel restaurant is a fallback after the preferable options have been ruled out.
Feedback A: A favourite first choice is not a last resort.
Feedback B: Randomly choosing between equally good options does not make one a fallback after alternatives fail.
Tip: L05.

S04. COME OUT OF LEFT FIELD
A. During a chat about dinner plans, someone suddenly asks whether you believe in ghosts. You react to the unrelated question.
B. During a chat about dinner plans, someone asks whether you prefer pasta or pizza.
C. You ask a friend to explain the dinner booking and they answer the exact question you asked.
Correcta: A.
Respuesta modelo: "That question came out of left field."
Explicación: The question is both unexpected and disconnected from the dinner conversation.
Feedback B: This question follows naturally from the topic; it is not an unexpected change of direction.
Feedback C: A directly relevant answer to your own question does not have the surprise or oddness expressed here.
Tip: L08. Out of the blue can overlap; this scene highlights the unrelated topic.

S05. ASAP
A. You ask someone to send a photo whenever they happen to remember, with no hurry.
B. Your taxi is waiting and you need the building number now. You ask your friend to send it quickly.
C. You need a document next Friday and want to make the deadline clear.
Correcta: B.
Respuesta modelo: "Could you send me the building number ASAP? The taxi's here."
Explicación: An immediate practical need makes as soon as possible appropriate.
Feedback A: ASAP introduces urgency that the relaxed request does not have.
Feedback C: ASAP means as soon as possible; it does not specify a deadline. Saying next Friday makes the deadline clear.
Tip: L03.

S06. NO WORRIES
A. You caused a small mistake and want to admit that it was your fault.
B. Your friend tells you they're really worried about a family problem. You want to show that you understand how they feel.
C. Your friend apologizes for arriving two minutes late. It hasn't caused a problem, and you want to reassure them.
Correcta: C.
Respuesta modelo: "No worries. We've only just got here."
Explicación: The speaker accepts a minor apology and reassures the friend.
Feedback A: My bad would admit responsibility. No worries normally reassures someone rather than owning your mistake.
Feedback B: 'No worries' can sometimes sound too casual or dismissive here. A more empathetic response would better match the speaker's intention.
Tip: L12.

S07. IT IS WHAT IT IS
A. The last train has left and it cannot be brought back. You accept the situation and start looking for another way home.
B. Your booking has a typo that the agent can fix immediately. You want to ask the agent to correct it.
C. You are choosing between two available trains and want to explain why one is better.
Correcta: A.
Respuesta modelo: "It is what it is. Let's see if there's a bus."
Explicación: The speaker accepts an unchangeable fact while still considering what to do next.
Feedback B: The stated goal is to correct a fixable problem. This resigned response would not make that request.
Feedback C: Comparing available choices is different from accepting a fact that cannot be changed.
Tip: L14.

S08. NOT GONNA LIE
A. You are giving evidence in a formal statement and need to acknowledge that an earlier statement was false.
B. Your friends disliked a film, but you want to casually admit you enjoyed it.
C. You have not seen the film and want to avoid giving any opinion about it.
Correcta: B.
Respuesta modelo: "Not gonna lie, I actually loved it."
Explicación: This introduces a candid, informal opinion, especially one that may surprise the listeners.
Feedback A: This casual expression is not an appropriate substitute for a clear, formal correction of a false statement.
Feedback C: It normally introduces an opinion or admission; it does not communicate that you have no view to offer.
Tip: L15.

S09. ON THE SAME PAGE
A. You and a teammate disagree about the deadline and have not resolved it.
B. You and a teammate are working on unrelated tasks and do not know each other's plans.
C. You confirm with a teammate that you both understand who is doing what and when it is due.
Correcta: C.
Respuesta modelo: "Great, we're on the same page. I'll send my part by Thursday."
Explicación: Both people share the relevant understanding of the plan.
Feedback A: The unresolved disagreement means they are not yet on the same page about the deadline.
Feedback B: Simply working at the same time does not establish shared understanding.
Tip: L18.

S10. BETTER SAFE THAN SORRY
A. Before editing a file, you save a backup even though you do not expect anything to go wrong.
B. You have already lost the only copy of a file and want to apologize for deleting it.
C. You decide not to make a backup because you are certain nothing can go wrong.
Correcta: A.
Respuesta modelo: "I'll save a copy first. Better safe than sorry."
Explicación: A small precaution now could prevent regret later.
Feedback B: The phrase justifies taking a precaution; it is not itself an apology for a loss that has already happened.
Feedback C: Skipping the precaution expresses the opposite approach.
Tip: L22.

S11. GO WITH THE FLOW
A. You insist that everyone follow your timetable exactly, even though the group wants to change it.
B. Your friends suggest changing the plan to something you don't feel comfortable doing.
C. You're on a relaxed day out with friends and are happy to adapt as the group's plans change.
Correcta: C.
Respuesta modelo: "I'm happy to go with the flow. Let's see what everyone feels like doing."
Explicación: The speaker willingly accepts flexibility instead of controlling every detail.
Feedback A: Insisting on a fixed timetable is the opposite of adapting to the group.
Feedback B: Going with the flow suggests willing flexibility. It doesn't mean ignoring your own boundaries or discomfort.
Tip: L23.

S12. CALL IT A DAY
A. You have just started studying and want to propose working for several more hours.
B. You've been practising for hours, you're tired, and you suggest stopping now and continuing tomorrow.
C. You want to quit your course permanently and explain that you will never return.
Correcta: B.
Respuesta modelo: "Let's call it a day. We can finish this tomorrow."
Explicación: The speaker proposes ending the current session rather than abandoning the activity permanently.
Feedback A: This expression would stop the session, not extend it.
Feedback C: Call it a day does not by itself clearly communicate permanently leaving a course. In this exercise it means stopping for now.
Tip: L25.

P1. Your friend texts you...

What does "figured out" mean here?
A. Found out where to buy a charger
B. Understood what the problem was
C. Stopped trying to fix the laptop
D. Asked someone else to repair it
Correcta: B. Understood what the problem was
Explicación: The loose cable was the explanation. Your friend understood the problem after investigating it.
Feedback A: There's no shopping here: the friend has identified why the laptop wasn't charging.
Feedback C: Finally and the explanation about the cable tell us the problem was solved, not abandoned.
Feedback D: The message describes understanding the cause, not asking someone for a repair.
Pitikoi Tip: You find out a fact, but figure out a problem. With it, say figure it out.

P2. A coworker catches you before you leave...

What does "found out" mean here?
A. Decided to move the meeting online

C. Learned that the meeting will be online
D. Started looking for an online meeting
Correcta: C. Learned that the meeting will be online
Explicación: Your coworker has learned a new fact about the meeting.
Feedback A: Learning about the change is different from making the decision to change it.
Feedback B: No technical problem is mentioned. The new information is that the meeting will be online.
Feedback D: Just found out tells us the information has already been discovered; the search is not just starting.
Pitikoi Tip: Find out means discover information. Figure out means understand or solve something by thinking.

P7. A friend calls from the roadside...

Feedback D: The message is about a faulty car, not a navigation mistake.
Pitikoi Tip: The context determines the meaning: a broken machine, a strong emotion or a step-by-step explanation.

PHRASAL — COMPLETE THE SENTENCE

P1. A noisy neighbour
Mia: Is your neighbour still playing drums at midnight?
Sam: Every night. I can't ___ that noise anymore.
A. keep up with
B. put up with
C. turn off
Correcta: B. put up with
Explicación: Sam is talking about tolerating an ongoing annoyance. Put up with fits that frustration.
Feedback A: Keep up with is about matching a pace or staying informed. Sam is describing noise they can no longer tolerate.
Feedback C: Turn off would mean stop a device. Sam cannot switch off a neighbour playing drums; the complaint is about tolerating the noise.
Pitikoi Tip: Put up with emphasizes tolerating an annoyance. Deal with can mean actively handling a problem. Keep the phrase together: put up with it.

P2. Catching up after a date
Alex: How was your date?
Jo: Really good. We ___ talking until three in the morning.
A. ended up
B. gave up
C. showed up
Correcta: A. ended up
Explicación: Ended up + -ing describes what eventually happened, often beyond the original plan.
Feedback B: Give up talking means stop talking. Until three describes how long the conversation continued.
Feedback C: Show up means arrive. Here Jo is describing how the evening developed, not their arrival.
Pitikoi Tip: Use end up + -ing: end up staying. You can also end up somewhere: We ended up at her place.

P3. Outside the station
Nina: Is that our taxi?
Leo: Yep. Let's ___ the car. The driver's waiting.
A. get on
B. get out of
C. get in
Correcta: C. get in
Explicación: For a car or taxi, the usual expression is get in. For a bus, train or plane, use get on.
Feedback A: Get on is normally used for buses, trains and planes. Here they are entering a car.
Feedback B: Get out of means leave the car. They are still outside, ready to enter.
Pitikoi Tip: Usually get in a car or taxi, but get on a bus, train or plane. To leave: get out of the car / get off the bus.

P4. On the bus

Feedback A: For this kind of transport, we normally say get off the bus. Get out of is usual for a car or taxi.
Feedback C: They are already on the bus; get on would mean board it.
Pitikoi Tip: Get on / get off go together. For cars, use get in / get out of.

Pitikoi Tip: You find out a fact, but figure out a problem. With it, say figure it out.
